Why it matters

One cold night can take a year's income

Warm spells wake buds early. Then a single freeze hits fruit that can't take it, and most farms still decide by the air temperature, not the bud.

71–86%

Crop lost in one event

B.C.'s January 2024 freeze cut production of peaches, nectarines, apricots, cherries and wine grapes by up to 86%.

1–2°C

How much colder buds run

On clear, calm nights buds lose heat to the sky and run colder than the air, yet fans and sprinklers start on an air-temperature rule.

21–39 ac

Average fruit farm

Typical B.C. and Ontario farms are small. A wind machine costs about $35,000, and sensor platforms charge $60–260 per acre each year.

Sources: Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, Statistical Overview of the Canadian Fruit Industry 2024; OMAFA, Wind Machines for Minimizing Cold Injury (2026); FAO, Frost Protection: Fundamentals, Practice and Economics (2005).

How it works

Predict the bud. Prevent the damage. Prove it worked.

01 · PREDICT

Forecast your buds, hours ahead

Solar posts measure the air, how cold the sky is, and a bud twin, an artificial bud that cools like a real one. FrostNet forecasts each block's bud temperature and sends a prepare-today list 24–48 hours before a frost night.

About one post per 5–7 acres
02 · COUNT DOWN

The Frost Canary counts down

Four tiny sealed water cells, each tuned with salt to freeze at a different temperature, freeze one by one before your buds are hurt. A wireless coil reads them, and every freeze corrects the forecast on the spot.

Nothing touches your trees
03 · PREVENT AND PROVE

Start on time, stop when safe

FrostNet starts your fans or water at the latest safe time, doses water to the heat being lost, checks that protection is working, and stops only when a reference post says it's safe. A morning report shows what happened.

Your manual override always wins

Why air temperature isn't enough

Buds get more fragile as they open

The same −2°C night can be harmless in early April and costly at full bloom. FrostNet tracks your crop's stage, and you click in the canary cartridge for that stage, so the countdown always ends before damage.

Precision Frost Shield

Water dosed to the frost, block by block

When water freezes it releases heat, 334 joules for every gram. Most sprinklers run at one fixed rate all night. FrostNet measures how fast each block is losing heat and pulses under-tree water to match it.

  • Works on drip and micro-sprinkler lines you may already have, through the FrostNet Valve Kit
  • Colder blocks get longer pulses; milder blocks get less water
  • The canary in each block checks that it's working, and FrostNet adds water or calls you if not

Pulsed watering is a new method. Our spring 2027 pilot tests it against continuous watering on matched blocks.

Questions growers ask
FAQ

    

      
Does FrostNet replace my wind machines or sprinklers?
No. It makes the equipment you already have work at the right time. FrostNet starts it at the latest safe time, checks that it's working, and stops it only when it's safe. With the Valve Kit, it can also dose water on drip or micro-sprinkler lines.

      
Does anything attach to my trees?
No. The Frost Canary sits on the post's short arm, a hand's width from the outer buds. It sees the same sky and air as your buds, so nothing needs to touch the plant.

      
What is the Frost Canary?
Four tiny sealed water cells. Salt sets each one to freeze at a slightly different temperature, for example −0.5, −1.0, −1.5 and −2.0°C. They freeze one by one as the night cools, and the last one always freezes before your buds' damage point. A coil in the canary head reads them without wires.

      
What happens if the internet or power goes down?
The gateway runs the start and stop rules on your farm, so it keeps working without internet, and it has a battery backup. Your fan's own thermostat autostart stays wired too. If in doubt, FrostNet starts early and calls you.

      
How is it installed?
The app reads your farm's elevation map and shows where cold air collects. You push the posts in where it shows, about one per 5–7 acres plus one reference post away from the fans, and scan each QR code. A dealer or electrician wires the relay box. Posts can stay out all year.

      
Who owns my farm's data?
You do. We use it to run your alerts and improve your farm's forecast.

      
Is FrostNet available now?
FrostNet is in development. We are recruiting five pilot farms for spring 2027. Figures on this site are design targets and estimates that the pilot will test.
